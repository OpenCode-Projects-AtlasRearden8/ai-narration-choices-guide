Chapter 2: Platform Rules, Compared in Plain Words

Rules change. This chapter is written so you re-verify numbers on the platform's own page before you sign. We name platforms because you'll meet them anyway — not because anyone paid us.

The vocabulary you actually need (Five terms)

  • PFH — Per Finished Hour. A narrator price quoted per final hour of audio. A 9-hour book at $250 PFH costs $2,250. You own the audio outright.
  • Royalty Share. No money up front; narrator and author split royalties. You don't pay cash, but the narrator is auditioning your willingness to promote as much as you're auditioning them, and you usually don't own the audio separately.
  • Exclusive vs non-exclusive. Whether you license the audiobook to one platform alone (bigger royalty rate, nowhere else) or several (smaller per-copy rate, everywhere).
  • Retail sample. The 5-minute clip shoppers can play before buying. You choose or approve it. It is the single most important marketing asset you control.
  • Whisper/listening time pay. Some subscription services pay per minute listened, not per sale. Royalty statements look weirder; expect smaller, steadier figures.

Platform-by-platform, in plain words

Figures below are typical as of late 2025 / 2026 and exist to help you compare shapes of deal, not to be your quote. Always confirm current terms on the platform's official page before signing.

Audible / ACX (Amazon)

  • The biggest listener base. Also the strictest packaging.
  • Distribution: Either exclusive (Audible/Amazon/iTunes only, higher royalty ~40%) or non-exclusive (also sell retail CDs/plugins elsewhere, lower royalty ~25%).
  • Payments: Royalty Share (50/50 split) or Pay Per Finished Hour (flat rate from $0–$250 depending on narrator tier; you pay narrator cash).
  • AI voices: ACX offers a limited "Virtual Voice" program: platform AI voices on books that already exist as ebooks, with a lower royalty rate than human narration, and you may not separately license the audio elsewhere. Historically ~25% royalty. Human-narrated exclusive is ~40%.
  • Specs: Retail-quality WAV masters, specific channel and noise rules, opening and closing credits read by the narrator ("This is [title], narrated by ...").
  • Plain-words verdict: Biggest reach, most paperwork. If you sign exclusive with a human on Royalty Share, you're committing to a two-year term — read that clause.

Findaway Voices / Spotify Audiobooks

  • Distribution: Non-exclusive network reach — the audiobook can flow to Spotify, libraries (OverDrive/Hoopla), Apple Books, Kobo, and dozens of small retail outlets from one upload.
  • Payments: You price the audiobook; platforms take a cut; you get paid per channel. No exclusivity games.
  • AI voices: Offers AI narration tiers alongside human narration hiring, clearly labeled in their catalog.
  • Plain-words verdict: Best "everywhere at once" non-exclusive option, and the least painful way to test a human narrator at mid-tier rates. Spotify listening is growing fast.

Google Play Books

  • Distribution: Non-exclusive.
  • AI voices: Has an auto-narration program — AI reads the audiobook from your existing ebook; you can audition/choose from several voices, and the audiobook is labeled as AI-narrated.
  • Plain-words verdict: Cheap experiment; production quality is "fine." Fewer audiobook listeners overall than Audible/Spotify.

Kobo / Rakuten, Apple Books, and direct stores

  • Usually non-exclusive aggregators (Findaway distributes to most of these for you).
  • Writing an entirely separate upload for each of these by hand is almost never worth your time — use an aggregator unless you're huge.

Your own site (Gumroad, Payhip, Ko-fi, etc.)

  • Non-exclusive, you keep ~90%+, zero platform rules.
  • Reality check: almost no one buys audiobooks this way because files are a hassle and there's no player. It's a good extra, a bad primary.

Three plain-words rules of thumb

  1. Exclusive is a bet on one platform. Bigger royalty per copy; smaller number of places to win. Non-exclusive is diversification. Most first-time authors on realistic budgets should think hard about earning an audiobook audience before signing an exclusive deal with any single store.
  2. AI narration is often restricted more tightly than human narration. If a platform's AI program looks generous, check (a) whether it limits which stores get the audio, (b) the royalty rate, (c) whether you can take the same audio files elsewhere.
  3. The retail sample is yours to choose or approve. Spend more effort choosing the sample chapter (usually the "first big grip" of the book) than on the cover.
