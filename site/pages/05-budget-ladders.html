Chapter 5: Budget Ladders

Five rungs, zero shaming. You climb as high as the book justifies — not as high as guilt suggests.

How to think about money here

An audiobook is a product you're packaging, not a vanity project. The right budget is the one where the book's likely annual earnings cover the production cost within a reasonable time (a rough self-publishing heuristic: production paid back inside 12–18 months at your typical listener volume). If your book sells 15 copies a year, dropping $3,000 on narration now is a bet your future self will wince at. That's okay. There's a rung on the ladder for that, too.

Per-finished-hour (PFH) math: multiply your word count by ~156–165 words per minute (a standard audiobook pace), divide by 60 to get estimated finished hours.

  • 30,000 words ≈ 3.2 hr
  • 60,000 words ≈ 6.5 hr
  • 100,000 words ≈ 10.5 hr

Use the exact estimate in Worksheet 3 so you stop scrolling forums and start comparing.

The ladder

Rung 0 — $0: DIY (flip to the companion handbook)

You read it yourself. Real cost is gear (a decent setup can be had under $150), room treatment (closets, spare bedrooms), and time — expect 2–3× the finished runtime to record, plus many more hours editing. Best for: author-on-camera brands, poetry, memoir, low-resource first books. Audiobook Recording Handbook covers the craft from macro (mic, room) to micro (breath editing).

Rung 1 — Under $250: AI voice (platform or dedicated tool)

Two flavors:

  • Platform AI voice. Google Play auto-narration, Findaway AI narration tiers, or ACX Virtual Voice (if your ebook already exists there). You choose from a catalog voice.
  • Third-party AI narration tool. Generate the audio yourself and upload as a produced audiobook (note: some platforms restrict third-party AI narration — see chapter 2); quality varies; disclosure applies.

Truth capsule: Expect a rubric score around 14–18 — strong polish, limited emotional range. Best for nonfiction, self-help, reference, essay. Disclose honestly.

Rung 2 — $250–$1,000: Early-career human narrator per-project

New-ish narrators with solid demos charge modest project rates (not strictly PFH). You're not getting the golden-voiced veteran — you're getting competence. Rubric target: 17–21. Pick from audition samples using chapter 3, and sign a clean 1-page deal as in chapter 4.

Rung 3 — $1,000–$2,500 total ($150–$350 PFH): Established human narrator

Standard PFH territory for reliable mid-career narrators. You own the audio outright. This is the "classic professionally-produced audiobook" tier. If your book earns $200–$1,000/month continuously, this is usually where the money goes first.

Rung 4 — $2,500+ (often per finished hour bid upward): Experienced, known, or union pro

Big-name narrators, dual- or multi-cast performances, union (SAG-AFTRA) hires. Mind the rate negotiation and do not sign royalty share at this tier — this is a work-made-for-hire zone. Get the rights cleared explicitly, sign the paper, record.

Budget-ladder worksheet (Worksheet 3)

Fill in your numbers before quoting anyone:

  1. Word count ______ × 160 wpm ÷ 60 = estimated finished hours ______
  2. Target rung (0–4): ______
  3. Estimated cost ceiling for that rung: ______
  4. Break-even check: monthly audiobook revenue you'd need to repay in 12 months = cost ÷ 12 = ______ — is that plausible against your ebook sales? Y / N
  5. If N at your target rung, drop one rung and repeat. Never skip to "AI because it's easier" without checking the platform rules first (ACX etc., see chapter 2).

If you can only afford rung 1 today

Publicly good advice, minus the shame:

  • Publish the AI-narrated edition at a normal price. Don't discount it to embarrassment-level; deep discounts mostly just teach shoppers to wait.
  • Add a line in your book description noting a second "human-narrated" edition is coming (if you plan one). This also creates a nice future launch moment.
  • Let this book's audiobook revenue fund rung 3 on the next book. That's the actual ladder mechanic.
