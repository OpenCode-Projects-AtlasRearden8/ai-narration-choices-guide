# Chapter 6: The Launch-Day QC Checklist

*Ninety minutes on publication day that protects the other few hundred you spent.*

---

> This checklist is also an interactive page on the site — keep it open on your phone
> while you work. Printable worksheet: Worksheet 4.

## A. Audio files, re-verified

- [ ] **Open and play the retail sample first.** Listen on earbuds *and* on phone
      speaker for real-listener realism. If the sample is 8 seconds of dead air before
      the first voice, fix it now — that's the #1 discard reason for shoppers.
- [ ] Spot-check **chapter transitions**: play the last 30 seconds of chapter 3 and the
      first 30 seconds of chapter 4 back to back. No lost words, no awkward silence
      lengths, no mismatch in volume.
- [ ] Check the **bookend credits**: "This is *[title]*, narrated by *[name or honest AI
      label]*" at the start and a matching close at the end. Required by most retail
      standards.
- [ ] Verify total runtime in your catalog/publish page matches what the audio actually
      is; platforms might flag and re-derive it, but yours should be intentional.
- [ ] Confirm **chapter metadata titles** ("Chapter One — The Letter", not "track7final").
      Chapter titles become your listener's scrubber UI.
- [ ] Confirm **bitrate / format / channel** specs match the platform requirements you
      signed for (typically 192kbps+ CBR WAV master for ACX, and platform-specific
      others; check your platform's current spec sheet).

## B. Metadata and rights

- [ ] **Narrator credit** is exactly right, spelled exactly, matching the audio's
      closing credit. "As narrated by" fields populate search filters on retail pages.
- [ ] **AI narration disclosure** is present if applicable, in the description
      (platform-labeled programs aside, you should also say it plainly in words you
      control).
- [ ] Copyright year and holder ("© 2026 Asdale Dynamics, LLC" pattern — yours, not
      ours) match across audio metadata, upload form, and any generated PDF booklet.
- [ ] The **sample chapter chosen** is a hook, not just "Chapter 1, verbatim, static" if
      your first chapter is slow. If the first scene is a slow burn, choose the sample
      by grip, not by order.
- [ ] Titles/series names match the ebook edition *exactly* (so shoppers' bookmarks and
      series pages line up).

## C. Platform and pricing

- [ ] **Pricing** cross-checked against your chosen rung from [chapter 5](05-budget-ladders.md)
      — don't underprice as panic; discounts later are cheaper than a permanent anchor.
- [ ] Pre-order vs launch-time decision made *deliberately* (and if pre-order, the
      upload is complete and QC'd *before* it goes live, not five minutes after).
- [ ] Exclusivity term you signed matches your distribution plan going forward — the
      audio and your sales strategy should share the same belief system.
- [ ] Every intended store (Audible + Findaway; or your chosen wide combination)
      actually shows a live listing, not a "processing" placeholder.

## D. The human test

- [ ] One person who didn't write the book listens to **10 minutes of the final retail
      sample** on whatever device they actually use and answers: "Would you keep
      listening?" If no, decide fast whether it's fixable by sample re-edit or by
      accepting it and moving on.
- [ ] You review the first **three customer reviews** within 48 hours of launch —
      narrations get reviewed in their own right; reader feedback on the voice is your
      rubric validation for the next book.

## E. Post-launch hygiene (within the first week)

- [ ] Add the audiobook edition to your website's book page and newsletter arc.
- [ ] Screenshot the live listing with its ASIN or equivalent for your own records,
      including "narrated by" display, in case terminology drifts on the platform side.
- [ ] If you're in [Worksheet 3](worksheets/ws-03-budget-ladder-planner.md), log the
      actual production cost — actual spreads of actual numbers are what make the
      *next* book's budget decision honest.

**If something fails:** don't panic-re-upload twenty times in an afternoon. Most
platforms queue successive uploads; the *fix* is one corrected file plus one calm
resubmission note, not a barrage.

---

That's the whole guide. May your launch go quietly, your audio pass QC first try, and
your sample be the hook it should have been.
